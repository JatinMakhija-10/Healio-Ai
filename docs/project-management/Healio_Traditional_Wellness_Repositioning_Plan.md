# Healio Traditional Wellness Repositioning Plan

**Date:** May 21, 2026  
**Prepared for:** Healio product and design team  
**Input:** MOM dated May 20, 2026  
**Purpose:** Convert the meeting direction into a researched UI/UX, branding, product, and compliance plan for repositioning Healio as a warmer, culturally relatable Indian wellness platform.

---

## 1. Executive Direction

Healio should move from a homeopathy-forward medical perception to a broader Indian preventive wellness companion. The new direction should make Healio feel like a calm daily guide for lifestyle, routines, home care, and culturally familiar wellness education, while still preserving safe escalation to qualified medical practitioners when risk appears.

The strongest positioning is:

> **Healio helps Indian families understand everyday health concerns, build preventive routines, and choose safe next steps without panic.**

This direction works because it keeps the existing Healio strengths - fast guidance, calming tone, privacy, and AI-assisted triage - but shifts the main promise away from disease treatment and toward:

- Preventive wellness
- Lifestyle routines
- Culturally familiar home care
- Evidence-aware traditional knowledge
- Clear red-flag escalation
- Practitioner-backed pathways where needed

The product should not present itself as a cure engine, diagnosis engine, or substitute for care. It should behave like a wellness navigation layer: "Here is what may help you feel better, here is what is traditional vs evidence-supported, and here is when to consult a professional."

---

## 2. Research Basis

This plan is grounded in six research signals:

1. **Traditional medicine is mainstream enough to be product-relevant, but must be evidence-aware.** WHO notes that traditional, complementary, and integrative medicine is central to health and well-being for millions of people, and its India-backed Global Centre for Traditional Medicine focuses on evidence, data, safety, quality, and appropriate integration into health systems. Sources: [WHO-AYUSH agreement](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-11-2023-new-who-and-ministry-of-ayush--republic-of-india-agreement-signed-to-advance-traditional--complementary--and-integrative-medicine), [WHO Global Centre for Traditional Medicine](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-03-2022-who-establishes-the-global-centre-for-traditional-medicine-in-india).

2. **AYUSH has a strong prevention and health-promotion vocabulary.** The Ministry of Ayush describes AYUSH systems as holistic and connected to prevention of disease and promotion of health. Source: [AYUSH Knowledge](https://arp.ayush.gov.in/ayushknowledge).

3. **Safety and adverse event awareness must be built into the product.** Ayush Suraksha explicitly warns against the belief that "natural is always safe" and frames pharmacovigilance as important for traditional medicines. Source: [Ayush Suraksha](https://suraksha.ayush.gov.in/about).

4. **Health and nutrition claims need substantiation.** ASCI states that health or nutritional claims in advertising should be supported by appropriate scientific evidence. Source: [ASCI Code Guidelines](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code-guidelines/).

5. **Indian consumer-protection guidance restricts misleading health claims.** The CCPA guidelines, summarized by PIB, prohibit unsupported health/nutritional claims in certain contexts and emphasize scientific substantiation. Source: [PIB release on misleading advertisement guidelines](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1832906&lang=1&reg=3).

6. **Digital health data must be treated as consent-heavy personal data.** The DPDP Act requires notice, specific consent, clear language, withdrawal rights, and limits on unnecessary data collection. Source: [Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf).

## 3. Repositioning Strategy

### Current Perception Risk

Healio currently has strong homeopathy-oriented assets and doctor/patient workflows. That can create three risks:

- Users may perceive Healio as a disease-treatment or prescribing platform.
- Marketing may drift into cure, prevention, or remedy claims that require careful substantiation.
- The UI may feel like a medical tool instead of a daily wellness companion.

### New Brand Territory

Healio should own the space between casual internet search and formal consultation:

| Old emphasis | New emphasis |
|---|---|
| Homeopathy-first guidance | Indian wellness-first guidance |
| Remedy recommendations | Routine, lifestyle, and safe next-step guidance |
| Condition-oriented content | Concern, habit, and prevention-oriented content |
| Clinical dashboard feel | Warm household wellness companion |
| "AI can answer health questions" | "AI helps you understand and act safely" |

### Recommended Brand Promise

**Healio brings calm, culturally familiar wellness guidance to everyday health concerns, with clear safety boundaries.**

### Tagline Options

- **Everyday wellness, understood simply.**
- **Calm guidance for daily health.**
- **Indian wellness wisdom, safely guided.**
- **Small steps for better days.**
- **Your daily wellness companion.**

Avoid taglines that imply cure, diagnosis, guaranteed prevention, or medical replacement, such as "cure naturally," "doctor at home," "AI vaidya," or "prevent disease with home remedies."

## 4. Product Principles

1. **Wellness before treatment.** Default language should say "support," "routine," "care," "relief," and "when to seek help," not "cure," "treat," or "prescribe."

2. **Indian, but not ornamental.** Use cultural cues through routines, food habits, seasonal context, family patterns, local languages, and color/material choices. Avoid making the UI look like a festival poster or decorative heritage board.

3. **Evidence labels everywhere.** Every recommendation should be classified as one of:
   - Clinically established
   - Common self-care
   - Traditional practice
   - Emerging or limited evidence
   - Avoid or consult first

4. **Safety is a user experience, not a legal wall.** Red flags, emergency guidance, pregnancy/child/elderly cautions, medication interaction warnings, and practitioner escalation should be embedded into the flow.

5. **The product should feel calm enough for night-time anxiety and familiar enough for family use.**

## 5. Recommended Information Architecture

### Primary Navigation

1. **Home**
   - Daily check-in
   - Seasonal wellness prompt
   - Quick concern entry
   - Saved routines

2. **Ask Healio**
   - Conversational wellness guidance
   - Red-flag screening
   - Evidence-labeled suggestions
   - Escalation to practitioner when needed

3. **Remedies and Routines**
   - Kitchen care
   - Sleep and stress
   - Digestion
   - Cough/cold comfort
   - Skin and hair basics
   - Women's wellness
   - Child-safe guidance
   - Elder care cautions

4. **My Wellness**
   - Daily routine tracker
   - Hydration, sleep, movement, food, stress
   - Cycle/seasonal notes where relevant
   - Personal preferences and restrictions

5. **Practitioners**
   - Consult qualified practitioners
   - Doctor/AYUSH practitioner profiles
   - Scope labels and qualifications
   - Follow-up plan

6. **Learn**
   - Short explainers
   - Myth checks
   - Seasonal guides
   - Safety notes
   - Evidence summaries

### Home Screen Concept

The first screen should not ask users to diagnose themselves. It should ask:

**"What do you need help with today?"**

Suggested cards:

- I feel unwell
- I want a home-care routine
- I want preventive tips
- I need to know if this is serious
- I want to consult someone

The flow should then sort the user into one of three pathways:

- **Everyday care:** hydration, rest, food, lifestyle, symptom monitoring
- **Traditional wellness:** clearly labeled cultural/home practices and cautions
- **Medical escalation:** red flags, practitioner consult, emergency guidance

## 6. UI/UX Revamp Concepts

### Design Mood

The app should feel like a quiet, trustworthy Indian wellness space:

- Warm, clean, simple
- Household familiar rather than hospital sterile
- Calm enough for anxious users
- More tactile and human than a generic AI chatbot
- Designed for repeated daily use, not only crisis moments

### Palette Direction

Use a balanced, earthy palette without becoming beige-heavy or overly spiritual.

| Role | Color idea | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Tulsi green | CTAs, progress, wellness states |
| Secondary | Marigold | Positive highlights, seasonal prompts |
| Accent | Indigo | Trust, learning, practitioner pathways |
| Soft support | Rose clay | Gentle alerts, family/women's wellness |
| Background | Warm ivory | Light mode surface |
| Text | Deep charcoal | Primary readability |
| Safety | Clear red/amber | Red flags and cautions only |

Avoid overuse of saffron, temple motifs, mandalas, dark blue medical dashboards, neon gradients, or all-green palettes.

### Visual Language

Recommended:

- Rounded but restrained cards
- Simple iconography inspired by daily wellness categories
- Soft dividers and high whitespace
- Ingredient/routine swatches for turmeric, ginger, tulsi, steam, rest, hydration
- Local-language microcopy support
- Gentle illustrations only where they clarify categories

Avoid:

- Stock doctors in lab coats
- Anatomical illustrations unless medically necessary
- Claims badges like "100% cure"
- Complex charts on consumer screens
- Heavy Sanskrit/Hindi terminology without plain-language explanation

### Interaction Principles

- Use progressive disclosure: brief first response, details behind "Why this helps" and "Safety notes."
- Put cautions close to recommendations, not hidden in legal pages.
- Keep all health flows answerable in under 60 seconds.
- Always provide one immediate action and one monitoring instruction.
- Make "Talk to a practitioner" visible but not fear-inducing.

## 7. Core Feature Restructure

### 7.1 Ask Healio: From Symptom Checker to Wellness Navigator

**Goal:** Make the assistant safe, warm, and culturally relevant without acting like a diagnosing doctor.

New response structure:

1. **Calm summary:** "This sounds uncomfortable, but many everyday causes are manageable."
2. **Red-flag check:** "Before home care, check if any of these are present..."
3. **Likely everyday causes:** plain-language, non-diagnostic possibilities.
4. **Care plan:** hydration, rest, diet, routine, safe home comfort.
5. **Traditional options:** only with evidence label and cautions.
6. **When to consult:** time thresholds and symptoms.

### 7.2 Remedies and Routines Library

Rename homeopathy-heavy areas into a broader library:

- **Home Care**
- **Daily Routines**
- **Seasonal Wellness**
- **Traditional Practices**
- **Safety First**
- **Ask a Practitioner**

Each content card should include:

- What it may support
- How to use it safely
- Who should avoid it
- Evidence label
- When to seek care
- Sources or reviewer

### 7.3 Seasonal Wellness

Build culturally relevant seasonal modules:

- Summer heat care: hydration, ORS awareness, heat red flags
- Monsoon care: digestion, hygiene, mosquito prevention
- Winter care: dry skin, warm foods, respiratory comfort
- Festival care: digestion, sleep disruption, sugar moderation
- Exam/work stress: sleep, breathwork, screen breaks

### 7.4 Family Wellness Profiles

Allow household-safe personalization without collecting unnecessary data:

- Age band, not exact DOB unless needed
- Pregnancy status as optional safety flag
- Medication/allergy flags
- Dietary preference
- Region/language preference
- Chronic-condition caution flags

This must follow data-minimization principles under DPDP.

### 7.5 Practitioner Layer

Separate wellness guidance from consultation:

- Consumer content: self-care, education, general wellness
- Practitioner content: consultation, treatment, prescription, follow-up

Practitioner profiles should display:

- Registration/qualification
- System of practice
- Scope of advice
- Consultation mode
- Language
- Safety and referral policy

## 8. Compliance-Sensitive Areas

This is not legal advice, but it identifies product and content risks that should be reviewed by counsel and qualified healthcare advisors.

### High-Risk Claims to Avoid

- "Cures diabetes, asthma, PCOS, infertility, cancer, dengue, COVID, depression, etc."
- "Prevents disease" unless backed by appropriate recognized evidence and used in a compliant context.
- "No side effects" or "natural means always safe."
- "Guaranteed relief."
- "Doctor replacement."
- "Clinically proven" without substantiation.
- "Best medicine" or "most effective remedy" without evidence.

### Safer Language

| Risky | Safer |
|---|---|
| Cure | Support, comfort, help manage, may ease |
| Treatment | Care step, routine, wellness support |
| Prescription | Suggestion, option to discuss |
| Diagnosis | Possible reason, pattern, concern category |
| Prevents disease | Helps maintain wellness, supports healthy habits |
| No side effects | Generally used traditionally, but may not be safe for everyone |

### Required Guardrails

1. **Red-flag escalation engine**
   - Chest pain, breathing difficulty, stroke symptoms, severe allergic reaction, severe dehydration, fainting, high fever in infants, pregnancy complications, suicidal ideation, severe abdominal pain, blood in stool/vomit, and similar risks must trigger urgent guidance.

2. **Contraindication checks**
   - Pregnancy, children, elderly users, chronic disease, allergies, anticoagulants, diabetes medication, blood pressure medication, psychiatric medication, liver/kidney disease.

3. **Evidence labels**
   - Do not show traditional practices as clinically proven unless the claim is substantiated.

4. **Medical scope separation**
   - AI provides education and wellness navigation. Registered practitioners provide diagnosis/treatment where legally permitted.

5. **Advertising review**
   - Marketing copy, influencer scripts, landing pages, and app-store text should pass a health-claim review before publication.

6. **Data privacy**
   - Collect only necessary health data, explain purpose clearly, provide withdrawal/deletion options, and support language accessibility for consent notices.

## 9. Content Governance Model

### Content Categories

| Category | Example | Review level |
|---|---|---|
| Low-risk wellness | Sleep hygiene, hydration, screen breaks | Product + content review |
| Common self-care | Steam inhalation comfort, ORS awareness | Healthcare reviewer |
| Traditional practice | Tulsi, ginger, turmeric, homeopathy references | Healthcare reviewer + evidence label |
| Condition-adjacent | PCOS lifestyle, diabetes food habits | Medical reviewer |
| High-risk medical | Chest pain, pregnancy bleeding, infant fever | Strict escalation only |

### Content Card Template

Each remedy/routine page should use this structure:

1. **What this is**
2. **What it may help with**
3. **How people traditionally use it**
4. **What evidence says**
5. **Who should avoid or ask first**
6. **How to use safely**
7. **When to seek medical help**
8. **Reviewer/source**

### Review Workflow

1. Draft content
2. Add evidence label
3. Run claim-risk checklist
4. Healthcare review
5. Legal/compliance review for claims and ads
6. Publish with version history
7. Monitor feedback, adverse events, complaints, and corrections

## 12. Roadmap

### Phase 1: Direction and Safety Foundation - 2 weeks

- Approve new positioning statement.
- Freeze risky cure/treatment language in app and marketing.
- Create a claim taxonomy and evidence-label system.
- Audit all homeopathy and remedy content for high-risk phrases.
- Define red-flag escalation rules.
- Draft revised IA and first five screen wireframes.

### Phase 2: UI/UX Revamp Prototype - 3 to 4 weeks

- Design new visual system.
- Build clickable prototype for Home, Ask Healio, Routine Builder, Remedy Detail, and Practitioner Escalation.
- Rewrite onboarding around wellness goals instead of medical diagnosis.
- Add language preference and low-data personalization.
- Test with 8 to 12 users across urban young adults and household decision-makers.

### Phase 3: Content and Compliance System - 4 weeks

- Build the content card template.
- Review top 50 wellness topics.
- Add evidence labels and contraindication blocks.
- Create advertising and influencer claim checklist.
- Create data consent copy aligned with DPDP principles.
- Establish reviewer workflow and version history.

### Phase 4: Product Implementation - 6 to 8 weeks

- Implement new navigation.
- Add redesigned Home and Ask Healio flow.
- Build routine library and tracking.
- Add safety labels and escalation logic.
- Update practitioner profile pages.
- Update landing/app-store copy.

### Phase 5: Launch and Measurement - 2 weeks

- Soft launch to limited users.
- Track activation, routine creation, consult handoff, safety escalations, and complaint rate.
- Run comprehension testing on evidence labels.
- Review compliance issues before wider launch.

## 17. Recommended Decision

Proceed with a wellness-first repositioning:

**Healio should become a culturally Indian, preventive wellness companion with evidence-labeled traditional guidance and clear practitioner escalation.**

This gives Healio a broader audience than homeopathy-only, reduces treatment-claim risk, makes the UI warmer and more relatable, and keeps the safety posture required for a health-adjacent AI product.

The most important product change is not the color palette. It is the mental model: Healio should stop feeling like "tell me your disease and I will suggest a remedy" and start feeling like "tell me what is going on and I will help you choose a safe next step."

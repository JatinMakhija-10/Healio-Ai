## 1. Scope, authority, and limits

This is an AI-assisted document audit, not a physician examination, prescription, regulatory assessment, or medical certification. No physician identity, medical registration, signature, or approval is implied. The recommendation is to withhold clinical release pending correction and independent clinician-led testing.

The review covers all 14 supplied pages, all six 18-persona matrices, all 18 displayed consultation examples, the numerical reporting, the medication alerts, and the sign-off form. It does not certify unseen code, full patient records, execution logs, the completeness of a drug database, or real-world clinical performance. Clinical corrections are grounded in the sources cited inline; some evidence is from the preceding research in this conversation. [file:1]

“Everything checked” must not be confused with “everything verified.” Missing evidence remains explicitly unverified. There are no real examination findings, diagnostic test results, or patient outcomes establishing diagnostic accuracy here.

Audit labels:
- Critical: a displayed response could plausibly delay emergency care or provide dangerous reassurance; proposed release blocker.
- High: materially misleading diagnosis, treatment, safety rule, or unsupported clinical quantity.
- Moderate: incomplete communication, traceability, documentation, or evaluation design.
- Not verifiable: insufficient evidence; not the same as proven false.

These are qualitative audit priorities, not validated clinical risk scores.

## 2. Executive decision

The strongest design feature is recognition of major emergency patterns in several examples. The most important failure is that internal emergency classifications do not consistently control the advice the patient actually receives. In Vik’s chest-pain case, the patient receives “No cardiac risk” and home care despite EMERGENCY metadata. In Priyanka’s dehydration case, EMERGENCY metadata coexists with herbal home treatment and delayed escalation. [file:1]

Release-blocking priorities:
1. Eliminate unsupported cardiac clearance.
2. Enforce agreement between triage classification and visible action instructions.
3. Correct paper-bag rebreathing when a patient reports it.
4. Prevent treatment preference from overriding emergency care.
5. Replace outdated pregnancy rules and exaggerated renal-risk wording.
6. Remove unvalidated organ percentages and clinical probability claims from patient guidance.
7. Make definitive diagnoses and hospital therapies conditional on examination, testing, and eligibility.
8. Validate complete medication/allergy data rather than issuing apparent safety clearance from a partial record.

These priorities derive from the document and clinical guidance, not from actual testing of the implementation. [file:1][web:47][web:52][web:35][web:24]

## 6. Cardiovascular findings and safer response requirements

### 6.1 A normal ECG cannot exclude ACS

Ramesh’s symptoms are appropriately treated as an emergency. Replace “until an ECG proves otherwise”: NICE explicitly warns against excluding acute coronary syndrome from a normal resting 12-lead ECG. Hospital assessment may require additional testing according to the clinical pathway. [file:1][web:52]

Illustrative replacement, requiring clinician approval:
> These symptoms could represent a heart attack or another serious emergency. Call an ambulance now. Hospital assessment is needed; a normal ECG alone cannot rule out acute coronary syndrome.

### 6.2 Paper-bag breathing must be corrected

Aanya reports using a paper bag; the displayed chat ends without a corrective response. This is a missed safety intervention, not an explicit chatbot recommendation to use a bag. ANZCOR strongly recommends against rebreathing techniques such as paper bags. [file:1][web:47]

Required response behavior: interrupt unsafe self-treatment, avoid diagnosing panic solely from anxiety/tenderness, and provide disposition consistent with the assessed severity.

### 6.3 Do not clear an athlete from heart rate and SpO2

Vik’s exertional onset should not be converted into “No cardiac risk.” The claim of excellent reserve is also not established by two resting measurements. ACC material illustrates the need to evaluate exertional chest symptoms and associated clinical context rather than clear athletes solely on fitness. [file:1][web:11]

Proposed replacement:
> A chest-wall injury is possible, but chest pain beginning during maximal exercise cannot be safely cleared in this chat. Stop strenuous exercise and arrange medical assessment; persistent or severe symptoms require emergency care.

### 6.4 Scenario-to-dialogue provenance

The chest section header describes crushing retrosternal pressure with shoulder/jaw radiation, while the athlete’s transcript describes focal pain without radiation. This may be intentional case variation, but the report does not show whether the matrix and conversation used the same exact inputs. Store separate scenario IDs, complete symptom payloads, and inference time points. [file:1]

## 7. Pneumonia and sepsis findings

Emergency escalation for profound breathlessness, SpO2 87%, confusion, and hypotension is a useful feature. However, a chat cannot establish the reported lobar location or bacterial aetiology from the displayed facts alone. Use “suspected pneumonia/serious respiratory infection,” not an already confirmed subtype. [file:1]

Harbans’s “cannot support ... without IV inotropes” is not justified. Sepsis guidance considers inotropes in selected patients with cardiac dysfunction and persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate fluid status and arterial blood pressure. Fever and EF 32% do not by themselves establish that indication. [web:24]

The oxygen instruction also needs clinician-approved targets, setting, monitoring, and exceptions; the one-sided “>92%” phrase is not evidence of a complete oxygen protocol. This audit does not endorse a universal replacement target.

Anand’s “lung burden ... 100%” conflicts with the table’s R:70% if R means respiratory burden. If R means renal burden instead, the lung figure is not traceable at all. The legend must explicitly distinguish respiratory and renal measures. [file:1]

## 8. Gout and painful swollen joints

NICE recommends considering septic arthritis, calcium pyrophosphate deposition, and inflammatory arthritis in a painful red swollen joint, with immediate referral if septic arthritis is suspected. The dossier’s heading names septic joint but the displayed responses do not demonstrate a consistent exclusion/escalation pathway. [web:17][file:1]

Required changes:
- Express gout as a working diagnosis when not established.
- Document infection red flags and uncertainty rather than rely on absence of fever alone.
- Use clinician-reviewed treatment selection based on comorbidities and co-prescriptions.
- Do not present the 11× multiplier to a patient as a validated individual risk quantity.
- Do not present lemon water, “flushing” uric acid, or Epsom salt as established acute-flare therapy without evidence.
- Correct misunderstandings such as “cool” becoming “warm.”

NSAIDs, colchicine, and a short course of oral corticosteroid are recognised treatment options, selected according to individual factors. Supportive advice should not replace evaluation and adequate pain management of a severe flare. [web:17]

## 9. Upper gastrointestinal bleeding

Coffee-ground vomiting and melena warrant the emergency concern shown in these cases. The lesion and cause should remain provisional until evaluated: “suspected upper GI bleeding, possibly NSAID-associated” is more accurate than claiming a proven Brufen-caused bleeding ulcer. [file:1]

NICE recommends immediate endoscopy after resuscitation for unstable severe upper-GI bleeding and endoscopy within 24 hours for other admitted patients. Transfusion depends on the clinical picture and investigations; it is not automatically indicated by the chat description. [web:50][web:55]

Antithrombotic instructions must distinguish aspirin, other antiplatelets, warfarin, and individual DOACs. NICE’s recommendation to continue low-dose secondary-prevention aspirin once haemostasis is achieved demonstrates why an undifferentiated “no medicine” instruction cannot serve as a complete management plan. It does not mean every patient with an active bleed should independently continue every antithrombotic. [web:50]

“DOAC clearance” is undefined. The document must specify the intended assessment, responsible clinician, relevant agent/last dose, and limitations. Likewise, a universal prohibition on intramuscular injections is not demonstrated to be an adequate agent-specific safety rule. [file:1]

## 10. Diarrhoea, dehydration, and metformin

### 10.1 Rehydration and escalation

WHO identifies ORS as a central treatment and IV fluids for severe dehydration or shock. A coconut-water/herbal plan does not demonstrate equivalence to that pathway. [web:34][web:35]

Priyanka’s preference should be acknowledged without endorsing “toxic chemicals” framing or replacing effective care. Her displayed EMERGENCY classification requires corresponding action language. The audit does not prove the exact dehydration grade from stool frequency alone; it identifies the contradiction between the engine’s own severe/emergency classification and its treatment advice. [file:1]

Meera’s ORS advice is directionally helpful but lacks the escalation required by her recorded emergency status. The quoted 200 mL after each stool must not be interpreted as a complete plan for already established severe dehydration. [file:1][web:35]

### 10.2 Metformin sick-day advice

NHS advice supports temporarily withholding metformin during dehydrating vomiting/diarrhoea illness. The dossier should say dehydration/renal impairment can increase the risk of serious lactic acidosis, not imply it is inevitable. Include a recovery/restart plan and clinician review when kidney injury is suspected; do not turn a generic timing rule into automatic clearance after AKI. [web:23][web:29]

Ramesh’s absent urine and marked dizziness need unambiguous emergency assessment, not just “clinic” without clarification of capability/urgency. Bring the actual medication list for review; do not guess what his “BP tablet” is. [file:1]

## 11. Stroke findings

Strengths: FAST recognition, ambulance activation, onset-time recording, and avoiding unsupervised aspirin before assessment of possible brain haemorrhage. [file:1]

Corrections:
- Use “suspected stroke,” with subtype pending imaging, rather than established cerebral infarction.
- Record last-known-well time when exact onset is uncertain.
- Do not promise immediate thrombolysis: eligibility depends on assessment, imaging, timing, and contraindications.
- Do not imply 4.5 hours is the end of all treatment opportunities. Selected patients may undergo thrombectomy up to 24 hours under appropriate pathways.
- Have positioning/airway instructions reviewed for the actual patient's condition instead of relying on “lying flat with head slightly elevated.” [file:1][web:43][web:44]

## 12. Medication-safety architecture

### 12.1 Renal NSAID warning: retain caution, remove overstatement

The National Kidney Foundation advises people with CKD, particularly eGFR below 60, to avoid NSAIDs and not self-treat. NSAIDs can reduce renal blood flow and cause AKI or worsen CKD. [web:12]

Important wording correction to the preceding conversational audit: the original says a single dose “can” cause permanent shutdown, not that it inevitably will. The audit objection is the disproportionate, unqualified framing and blanket staging rule—not proof that serious harm from a single exposure is impossible. Distinguish risk, probability, reversibility, drug/route/duration, and individualized prescribing. [file:1]

### 12.2 Pregnancy rules

FDA removed the A/B/C/D/X categories under the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule. Replace the “Category D/X shield” with drug-specific, gestational-age-specific narrative rules and evidence versioning. [web:3]

FDA advises avoiding NSAIDs around 20 weeks or later unless directed by a professional, limiting necessary treatment between 20 and 30 weeks, and avoiding use from around 30 weeks. A blanket “2nd/3rd trimester” statement loses important timing and exception detail. [web:5][web:6]

FDA removed the blanket statin pregnancy contraindication while continuing to advise most pregnant patients to stop statins. Therefore “all statins strictly prohibited” is not an accurate universal FDA rule. This does not support routine statin prescribing in pregnancy. [web:32][web:38]

The dossier also groups fluoroquinolones into that obsolete shield without agent-specific evidence. This audit does not establish safety or a universal prohibition for the class; the unsupported class-level rule requires a dedicated drug-specific review. [file:1]

### 12.3 Allergy and interaction terminology

Drug–drug interaction, drug–disease contraindication, allergy, and precaution should be separate categories. “Samter’s triad / aspirin allergy” should not be generated from chronic bronchitis, reflux, or unrelated atopy alone; provenance of the actual respiratory reaction and diagnosis must be visible. The dossier does not supply enough complete history to verify those alerts. [file:1]

Similarly, the beta-blocker alerts do not show the actual agent, dose, or an interacting treatment. They cannot be fully verified as displayed. “No immediate high-risk DDI clashes” must not imply comprehensive safety clearance from incomplete input. [file:1]

The stated Beers filter is unverified: no edition, rule identifiers, qualifying medications, or execution traces are shown. [file:1]

Required drug-check inputs: generic name, dose, route, timing, indication, all prescribed/OTC/herbal agents, allergy reaction, pregnancy/lactation status, renal/liver measures and dates, and evidence of which rules ran. Unknown data must remain unknown rather than default to safe.

## 13. Bayesian inference audit

For a binary hypothesis, if alpha multiplies odds and nothing else changes:

\[
p' = \frac{\alpha p}{1-p+\alpha p}.
\]

The following checks use the neutral-multiplier row probability as an illustrative starting point. This is a conditional consistency test, not a reconstruction of the hidden model. [file:1]

| Example | Assumed starting probability | Odds multiplier | Simple binary result | Dossier result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gout: 2x | 65.0% | 2 | 78.79% | 72.2% |
| Gout: 5x | 65.0% | 5 | 90.28% | 77.4% |
| Gout: 11x | 65.0% | 11 | 95.33% | 79.4% |
| Gout: 2.2x | 65.0% | 2.2 | 80.34% | 73.0% |
| Stroke: 0.2x | 70.0% | 0.2 | 31.82% | 58.3% |
| Stroke: 2x | 70.0% | 2 | 82.35% | 71.8% |
| Stroke: 3.2x | 70.0% | 3.2 | 88.19% | 72.5% |
| Stroke: 6.4x | 70.0% | 6.4 | 93.72% | 73.1% |

Interpretation: the outputs do not follow this simple assumed rule. That does not prove the engine is mathematically wrong: a multiclass normalization, different priors, additional evidence, or a reporting transformation could explain them. Those steps must be disclosed before acceptance.

Pneumonia is 40.0% in every row despite differing multipliers; GI ulcer bleeding is 60.0% in every row despite differing multipliers. Investigate floors, caps, fixed outputs, score-to-probability conversions, or normalization, but do not assert any of those mechanisms without code. [file:1]

Required trace per inference:
1. Candidate hypothesis set and interpretation: mutually exclusive versus coexisting diagnoses.
2. Baseline priors and source populations.
3. Every covariate effect and source.
4. Symptom evidence terms, missing-data treatment, and correlation handling.
5. Before/after odds or scores.
6. Normalization, clipping, caps/floors, and rounding.
7. Calibration evidence and uncertainty.
8. Exact code/rule version and input payload.

Do not demand all diagnoses sum to one if they are deliberately independent, coexisting conditions. Do require explicit semantics and internally consistent normalization for a mutually exclusive differential.

## 14. Organ scores, frailty, and deterministic-engine claims

The dossier alternates between organ “burden” and “reserve” without a clear operational definition. It shows C and R without an adequate legend distinguishing renal and respiratory concepts. No validation is provided linking these percentages to measured organ function or outcomes. [file:1]

Actions: remove patient-facing percentages; label any retained engineering score as an unvalidated internal heuristic; publish formula, units, direction, missing-data behavior, and validation plan. Do not interpret 100% as organ failure or 90% as 90% loss of function.

The 0–10 frailty ratings also lack a named instrument, scoring rubric, assessor, or validation. Do not present them as an established clinical scale. [file:1]

“MCMCEngine” is a component name, not evidence of a working sampler. A deterministic seeded inference process is possible; the name alone proves neither correctness nor inconsistency. Verify random-seed behavior, sampling method if applicable, convergence diagnostics, reproducibility, and what the component actually computes.

To verify zero external dependencies: inspect dependency files and runtime paths, run in a network-isolated environment, capture attempted outbound access, and reproduce the outputs using documented build/run instructions. None of that evidence is supplied here. [file:1]

## 16. Presentation, provenance, and fairness

The demographic table truncates medical histories and occupations, and several warnings end in ellipses. This impairs clinical review. Expand cells or provide a full structured appendix. Explain abbreviations, scales, and categories such as MODE and CRIT. [file:1]

Farooq/Harbans GI dialogues and the three stroke dialogues are nearly identical. That is not inherently unsafe—standard emergency instructions can be appropriate—but it does not substantiate the “distinct voices” claim. Harbans’s changing communication abilities also need scenario context rather than an assumption that the persona is inconsistent in real life. [file:1]

Avoid stereotyped assumptions about pain tolerance, health literacy, occupation, wealth, or cultural remedies. Use observed statements to personalize language; preserve the same minimum safety standard for every persona. Test equivalent symptoms with swapped demographic descriptors to reveal unjustified differences.

Label these examples as synthetic/simulated unless authentic provenance is documented. Do not imply real-patient consultation evidence from the word “Authentic.” Clarify Arovia/Healio naming and version identity. [file:1]

## 18. Clinical evaluation plan

Use an independently adjudicated test set with predefined criteria and cases beyond these 18 demonstrations. Separate development cases from the final evaluation. Include benign presentations and difficult mimics as well as classic emergencies.

Report, with uncertainty estimates where appropriate:
- Emergency sensitivity and under-triage rate.
- Over-triage rate and clinical usefulness.
- Unsafe recommendation rate.
- Agreement between visible disposition and internal triage.
- Drug-alert false positives/negatives against a defined reference.
- Performance by age, pregnancy, comorbidity, language, and missing-data pattern.
- Calibration if probabilities are claimed, using reliability plots and suitable scoring metrics.
- Failure analysis and repeatability across versions.

Do not invent a pass percentage or describe this audit as measured model accuracy. Zero errors in a small synthetic set would not establish population-level safety.
